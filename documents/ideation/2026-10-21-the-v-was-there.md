# The V Was There: Nature Outside the Super Instance, and Rainbows

*Ideation note — Casey DiGennaro, 2026-10-21, as told to Muse. Fifth
in the day's series; a boundary correction to the earlier notes.*

## The V is not ours

The V of the birds is part of the **state of nature**. It is part of
something outside my super instance. **It was there whether I observed
it or not.**

That is fundamentally different from something you categorise and
declare and have a word for — a thing you must make an *agreement*
with somebody else that it is that thing. Categories live on the
agreement side. The V does not. The V was an indicator that those
birds had swum across the lake whether you understood it, observed it,
or even cared.

So the anatomy has an outside: a natural state that carries its own
traces, observer-independent. No God's-eye view never meant no world.
It means the world is there, written in wakes and wet ground — and
every reading of it is somebody's.

## The rainbow: objective, and only yours

Take it a step further. There are perspective-driven things that are
not less real for being perspectival.

A rainbow appears in a different place to me than to a person a
stone's throw away. You cannot catch up to the end of your own
rainbow. And the deeper point: **the rainbow is in your own eye.** Go
out to the water droplets and *every droplet has the complete rainbow
in it*. It is your perspective that has your rainbow — and it is only
your rainbow.

The substrate is complete and physical, everywhere, for everyone. The
phenomenon is constituted at a viewpoint. Both are true at once, and
neither cancels the other.

## The three layers, stated

1. **Nature's state** — traces that exist whether or not anyone reads
   them (the V, the wet ground). Outside every super instance.
2. **Perspectival phenomena** — real, physically grounded, and existing
   only at a viewpoint (the rainbow in your eye; the complete rainbow
   latent in every droplet).
3. **The super instance** — your simulation: readings of the first,
   structured by the geometry of the second, extended by agreements
   with others — and never to be confused with either.

## A third category: the objective-individual

The rainbow sits in a category that is neither subjective nor
objective. Call it the **objective-individual** experience.

It is not subjective: it is fully caused, law-bound, checkable — the
geometry holds for anyone who stands anywhere. It is not objective in
the usual sense either: you and the person beside you are *not
observing the same thing*. Each rainbow is instantiated at one eye,
from one position, and no two observers' rainbows coincide.

And yet you can *relate* — point, describe, compare, agree that both
are there. That relatability-without-identity is exactly what makes
it a unique window into how two selves can truly be in the same
reality with different perspectives: same world, same laws, same
moment — different phenomenon, honestly held by each.

Most of what one self knows of another's world is like this. The
ledger can align what we *declare*. The rainbow is the standing proof
that alignment was never the same as sameness — and never needed to
be.

## The word: objective intelligence

*(Added later the same day, after the term above was tried and found
incomplete.)*

"Objective-individual" didn't sit right — it still parked the rainbow
on the subjective–objective axis, and it had no time in it. The fuller
account: the V **persists** — a trace the water keeps, rewindable,
readable by anyone while it decays; that is objective reality. The
rainbow **does not persist**. Rain falls as separate droplets, each
flashing a complete spectrum for an instant; the eye hooks those dots
faster than noticing and extrapolates them into one continuous
waveform. A photograph ten minutes later differs — sun moved, cloud
moved, droplets replaced, and the photographer is a different sampler
for having taken the first. You cannot step into the same rainbow
twice, and neither can anyone else — two cameras side by side land
their bows on slightly different spots.

Casey's resolution of the word-hunt: *the concept doesn't really exist
yet — and I like it.* **There is such a thing as objective
intelligence.** There is subjective intelligence, of course. But a
rainbow is an example of an **objective intelligence**: an act of
integration — dots made into a coherent bow — that no single party
performs or owns. The coherence is made by the configuration itself:
sun, rain, and an eye at a station. It is real, it is lawful, it is
available to any sampler at any station, and the master copy exists
nowhere.

That is the class the earlier sections were reaching for. The rainbow
is not an object and not an impression. It is an intelligence,
objectively at work in the arrangement of the world, rendered at a
point of view.

## Coda: an intelligence requires someone to explain it to

*(Same evening.)*

Which says something about what intelligence means. It has to do with
the waveform moving past your eyes as droplets, and something turning
it into the *concept* of a rainbow. You would not have that concept
without something that is **rendering it for something else**.

That is the key: the whole **iterator / iteratee** thing, in flow. An
intelligence requires that pairing. It requires the need for a
concept — and what requires a concept is **someone to explain it to**.

Intelligence, on this account, is never a solitary possession. It is a
rendering made for a receiver: droplets rendered as a bow, for the
rest of the self downstream of the eye; readings rendered as a story,
for whoever the story is told to; a simulation rendered as an
agreement, for the other party to the seam. Remove the iteratee and
the rendering has no reason to happen. The concept exists because
there is someone — another self, another cell, or your own future
self, who will pull the restamped memory off the drive — for it to be
explained to.

## Coda 2: sound into words — the same thing, turned on its side

Sound into words is exactly the other thing, turned on its side.
Where the rainbow integrates droplets *across space* into a bow,
hearing integrates **noise over time** — a waveform, transcribed,
somehow converted into concepts.

And it starts older than words. It starts with hearing a sound in the
woods and running away, because you are not going to be eaten — or
running *toward* something, to eat it. That creates **the initial
loop**: sample, render a concept (flee it / eat it), act — and the
acting changes what gets sampled next. The first iteratee of the
rendering was the body itself: the concept was made *for* the legs.

Words are that loop, refined until the renderings could be sent —
noise over time, made into concepts portable enough to cross a seam
and be rendered again in somebody else.

## Coda 3: the arc — same conceptualisation, further out each time

From there, sound only gets easier. Animals want to warn each other —
it saves their lives, or gets more food — so the rendering crosses to
other iteratees. Humans take it to the nth degree: they learn to
**code** with it (language), then leap with **writing**, further with
**movable type**, then **computers** — and now **automated iterators**.

But it is all the same conceptualisation idea, externalised one step
further each time:

- **Voice** — a rendering for iteratees within earshot; gone as it is
  made, like the rainbow.
- **Writing** — a rendering that *persists*, like the V: the evanescent
  made into a trace, readable by iteratees not yet born.
- **Movable type** — a rendering replicated: one conceptualisation,
  thousands of identical crossings.
- **Computers** — the rendering itself mechanised.
- **Automated iterators** — the other end mechanised too: loops
  rendering concepts for loops, the iterator/iteratee flow running
  with no animal in the middle at all.

The loop never changed. Only its reach, its persistence, and who — or
what — sits at each end.
